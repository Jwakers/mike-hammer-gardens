# Local SEO playbook

This is the practical local-search routine for Mike Hamer Gardens. Keep it
small: maintain the core business details, collect genuine reviews and reuse
real project photos. Do not create a blog or duplicate town pages unless search
data later shows a clear opportunity.

## Canonical business details (NAP)

Use these details consistently on the website, Google Business Profile,
Facebook and directory listings:

- **Name:** Mike Hamer Gardens
- **Location:** Gloucestershire
- **Phone:** 07715 978631
- **International phone:** +44 7715 978631
- **Website:** https://www.mikehamergardens.co.uk/
- **Email:** mikehamergardens@gmail.com
- **Hours:** Monday to Friday, 8:00am–4:00pm
- **Service area:** Gloucestershire. The website lists towns and villages
  across Gloucester, Cheltenham, Stroud, Tewkesbury, Cotswold and the Forest
  of Dean. The canonical list is `serviceAreaGroups` in `src/config/site.ts`.

Mike is a service-area business. Keep a residential street address hidden
unless he explicitly decides to publish business premises.

## Google Search Console

The site is live on the `www` hostname. The non-`www` and HTTP versions redirect
to it, and the live robots file allows crawling.

- Keep `https://www.mikehamergardens.co.uk/sitemap.xml` submitted.
- Check that `/` and `/gallery` are indexed. Request indexing only when needed.
- Review **Performance → Search results → Queries** and **Pages** quarterly.
- Compare the last three months with the previous period.
- Note local queries that already receive impressions, especially combinations
  of a real service and Gloucestershire or a town in the county.
- Only create a new service page when query data and genuine project evidence
  support it. Do not create near-identical pages for every village.

## Google Business Profile one-pass audit

- Business name is exactly **Mike Hamer Gardens**; do not add keywords.
- Primary category is **Landscaper** if available and accurate.
- Use additional categories only for services Mike genuinely provides.
- Profile is configured as a service-area business with the private address
  hidden.
- Phone, website and hours exactly match the canonical details above.
- Service areas match the website and are updated in both places together.
- Services match the current website service list.
- Description naturally mentions Gloucestershire without repeating keywords.
  Google Business Profile accepts fewer place names than the website. Prefer
  the county where the profile allows it, and otherwise the largest towns from
  the website list.
- Use a strong completed project as the cover image.
- Upload at least ten genuine, clear project photos initially.
- Add the short Google review link to the review-request message below.
- Answer genuine customer questions clearly; do not invent customer questions
  or reviews.

Suggested profile description:

> Mike Hamer Gardens provides landscaping, patios, fencing, turfing, garden
> clearance and reliable garden maintenance across Gloucestershire. With 13
> years' industry experience, Mike offers direct communication, tidy
> workmanship and practical advice from the first conversation through to the
> finished job.

## Reviews

Send this once after a completed job:

> Hi [first name], thanks again for choosing Mike Hamer Gardens. If you were
> happy with the work, would you mind leaving a short Google review? It really
> helps a small local business. If useful, you could mention the type of work
> and your area, but only share what you are comfortable making public:
> [GOOGLE REVIEW LINK]

Do not offer discounts or rewards for reviews. Do not ask customers to use
specific keywords.

Reply to every review. Keep replies short and specific:

> Thanks, [first name]. I’m glad you’re pleased with the [job type]. It was a
> pleasure working on your garden.

Use a town in the reply only when the reviewer has already made it public.

## Photos and project details

Mike’s only recurring task is to send one or two photos after a good job,
together with:

- permission to publish;
- the town or village, if the customer is comfortable sharing it;
- the work completed;
- whether the image is before, during or after.

Each quarter, upload the best photos to Google Business Profile. Add them to the
website gallery when they expand the range or quality of work shown. Never add
a location to a caption or image description unless it is confirmed.

Initial GBP photo shortlist already used on the website:

- tiered garden with turf, gravel and steps;
- flagstone patio and retaining wall;
- rebuilt dry stone wall;
- timber sleeper raised beds;
- fencing and raised beds on a slope;
- fresh turf lawn;
- cleared garden border;
- stone patio overlooking the hills;
- raised stone patio;
- before-and-after fencing project.

Use the original image files rather than screenshots from the website. Confirm
that every image shows Mike’s work before uploading it.

## Lightweight quarterly routine

Allow roughly 30–45 minutes:

1. Check GBP phone, hours, website and service areas.
2. Reply to any unanswered reviews.
3. Add one or two strong recent project photos.
4. Check Search Console queries and pages.
5. Fix any inconsistent details found on Facebook or directories.
6. Optionally publish one useful seasonal GBP update using an existing photo.

Possible seasonal updates:

- spring garden clearance and turfing;
- summer fencing, patios and regular maintenance;
- autumn hedge cutting and clearance;
- winter repairs, pressure washing and project planning.

## Citations

Create or correct only a small set of useful profiles:

- Facebook;
- Bing Places;
- Yell;
- Thomson Local;
- FreeIndex.

Use the canonical NAP details exactly. Skip paid directories unless Mike already
uses them and receives worthwhile enquiries. Do not create dozens of low-value
profiles.

## When to invest further

Consider one dedicated service page only when at least one of these is true:

- Search Console shows repeated impressions for a specific service query;
- Mike has several good examples and photos for that service;
- enquiries repeatedly ask for that service.

Good candidates might include dry stone walling, patios or fencing in
Gloucestershire.
Keep the page useful and evidence-led. A blog, weekly posting schedule, video
production and separate village landing pages remain out of scope unless the
contract grows.
